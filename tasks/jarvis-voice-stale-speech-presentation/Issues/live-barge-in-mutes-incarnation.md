# Live barge-in mutes the surface until the incarnation ends

- Found: Slice 00 audit, 2026-09-28, at `202333d`.
- `RealtimeAudioBridge._barge_in` calls `suppress_playback_until_session_end()` on Live (`jarvis/runtime/realtime_audio.py:1762-1764`); `LiveFrontendSession` then drops every later output of that session (`jarvis/runtime/live_frontend_session.py:365-373`).
- Effect: after one Live barge-in, whatever the scheduler dispatches (including a speech the brain re-emits under Slice 04) is inaudible until a reconnection. Probable contributor to "Jarvis stops answering" reports, and it interacts with the queue freeze of Slice 05.
- Owner: Slice 05 freshness-check decides whether it is handled there (reconnect or re-arm playback on the next addressed turn) or stays an Issue for a follow-up handoff.

## Slice 05 decision (2026-09-29): stays an Issue, not handled in Slice 05

Freshness-check at `c9ac376`: unchanged. `_barge_in` latches `suppress_playback_until_session_end()` first thing (`jarvis/runtime/realtime_audio.py`, `_barge_in`, right before the new `voice.floor.taken` relay); `LiveFrontendSession.cancel_output` does the same (`live_frontend_session.py:357-373`); `_legacy_events` drops every assistant PCM while latched (`:461`), `_output_was_interrupted` treats every output as interrupted (`realtime_audio.py:1720`), `output_started` is ignored (`:4073`).

Why not re-arm on the next addressed turn / reconnect here:
- The latch is a deliberate safety contract, not an oversight. Its docstring: "No primary Live boundary distinguishes a new answer from an old tail … Only a new session after confirmed close may play; input, transcript, delegation and append ACK cannot clear this latch." `tests/unit/test_live_runtime_safety.py:160-188` asserts exactly that: after a new user input (`UserTranscriptDelta`) and new local output ids, nothing is written to the device and the latch holds. Re-arming on an addressed turn would break that test by design and could make the interrupted answer's tail audible again — the defect the latch exists for.
- A correct fix needs either a provider boundary GPT-Live does not expose (an output id that provably starts after the cut), or a controlled re-incarnation (close + reopen the Live session after a barge-in, keeping conversation state), which is a lifecycle change of `PersistentVoiceRuntime` with cost/latency effects that only a real Live session can validate. Both are outside Slice 05's contract (queue freeze in the mouth).
- Interaction with Slice 05, measured by reading the code: the freeze itself is surface-independent and correct on Live; what it releases (noise ⇒ resume, timeout) is dispatched into the muted surface — the append happens, no audio is observed, and Slice 02 releases it `unconfirmed` after `LIVE_FIRST_AUDIO_TIMEOUT_S`. So on GPT-Live, after a barge-in, Jarvis stays silent until the incarnation ends: this satisfies the Slice 05 acceptance ("the first speech heard answers what the user said, or Jarvis stays silent") but HV-VOICE-STALE-05 (b) "Jarvis resumes what it had to say" **cannot pass on GPT-Live** until this Issue is fixed; it can on the Realtime surface.
